# Gibraltar Censuses – Historical Account

Source: Gibraltar National Archives – [Census Historical Account](https://www.nationalarchives.gi/PDF/InhabitantsRegisters/CensusHistoricalAccount181920.pdf)

## The Eighteenth Century

The earliest surviving census of the whole civilian population is that of **1753**.

Results (1753):
- British: 434 (or 414 in some accounts)
- Genoese: 597
- Jews: 575 (or 572)
- Spaniards: 185
- Portuguese: 25
- **Total civilian population: approx. 1,793**

By **1767** the population was classified by religion: British Protestants, Roman Catholics and Jews. This remained the standard classification for the rest of the 18th century.

**1777** census (still extant):
- Total: 3,201
- Protestant ("British Blood"): 506
- Roman Catholics: 1,832
- Jews: 863
- Natives: 1,334

The 1777 list was alphabetical by religion and recorded: surname, first name, age, country of birth, occupation, years in the Garrison, and remarks.

## Nineteenth Century

The earliest 19th-century census that has survived is **1814** (population 10,136). Growth was driven by Gibraltar being the only open port in Europe for British goods during the Napoleonic wars.

The modern decennial census dates from the **Census of the Population Ordinance, 1868**. Printed reports begin with the 1871 census.
